eBay Setup

Account Creation

  1. Go to ebay.com → Register (or sign in to existing account)
  2. Click "Sell" — eBay prompts first-time seller setup
  3. Identity verification — name must match your government-issued ID exactly. May request additional documents.
  4. Link bank account — Account Settings → Payments. eBay sends two microdeposits to verify.
  5. Tax info (W-9) — provide SSN or EIN. Required before payouts.
  6. Managed Payments activates automatically — no PayPal option. All funds route through eBay → your bank.
New Seller Hold
Plan for up to 21-day payout holds on your first sales. Uploading tracking promptly shortens holds to ~3 business days after delivery. Holds reduce after ~90 days, 25+ transactions, $250+ total sales.

Mercari Setup

Account Creation

  1. Download Mercari app (iOS/Android) or go to mercari.com
  2. Sign up with email, Google, Facebook, or Apple ID
  3. Phone verification — U.S. mobile number required (no VoIP). One account per person. Must be 18+
  4. Add profile photo and bio for buyer trust
  5. Add a credit or debit card (may be required before listing)
  6. Link bank account for direct deposit, or debit card for Instant Pay
  7. ID verification may trigger for $500+ items — complete early to avoid payout delays

Fees

Buyer protection fee (paid by buyer)~3.6%

Listing Process

  1. Tap "Sell" button
  2. Photos — up to 12. Front, back, corners/edges. Use your lightbox.
  3. Title — keyword-rich: [Year] [Brand] [Set] [Player] [Card #] [Grade]
  4. Description — condition, flaws, year, brand, set, card number
  5. Category — Sports → Trading Cards
  6. Price — factor in 10% fee. Enable "Offers" for negotiation
  7. Shipping — prepaid label (Mercari handles) or ship on your own

Key Features

  • Smart Pricing — auto-adjusts price based on demand (never below your floor)
  • Offers to Likers — private 10%+ discount to people who liked your item. Best for items sitting 30+ days
  • Payouts — Standard ACH: 2–5 business days (free). Instant Pay to debit: ~$3 fee, funds in minutes
  • Auto-complete — if buyer doesn't rate within ~3 days, transaction auto-completes and you're paid
Mercari Strategy
Price 10–15% below eBay comps — Mercari buyers expect deals. Relist items every 2 weeks to reset search algorithm visibility. Use all 12 photo slots.

Facebook Marketplace

Setup

  1. Use your personal Facebook account — navigate to Marketplace
  2. Click "Create new listing" → "Item for sale"
  3. Set up Commerce Profile when prompted (name, payout info for shipped items)
  4. Link bank account or PayPal for payouts on shipped sales

Fees

  • Local pickup: 0% — completely free
  • Shipped items (via Checkout): ~5% ($0.40 minimum)
  • Listing fees: None

The Real Play: BST Groups

Join 5–10 active sports card Buy/Sell/Trade groups. Search for "sports cards BST", "baseball cards for sale", "[your city] sports cards".

  • Read group rules first — most require price, condition, shipping terms
  • Post format: clear photo + "FS" (For Sale) + price + shipping info
  • Payment: PayPal Goods & Services (buyer protection) or cash for local
  • No platform fees if payment is handled outside FB Checkout
Important Change
Prepaid shipping labels are gone as of Feb 2025. You must buy your own label (use Pirate Ship for cheapest rates). Mark as shipped + enter tracking within 3 days or Facebook auto-cancels the order. Payouts take 2–3 weeks.
Safety
Meet at police stations or banks for local sales. Never accept Zelle, CashApp, or PayPal Friends & Family from strangers (no protection). Ship only to the address on the PayPal G&S payment.

Listing Workflow

eBay Single-Card Listing (Step by Step)

  1. Research value — eBay → Advanced Search → check "Sold listings". Also check 130point.com for actual Best Offer prices eBay hides.
  2. Start listing — Seller Hub → Listings → Create Listing
  3. Select category — Sports Mem, Cards & Fan Shop → Sports Trading Cards → [Sport] → Singles
  4. Fill item specifics — Sport, Player, Team, League, Set/Brand, Year, Card Number, Condition, Graded (Y/N), Autographed (Y/N). Fill EVERY field — more specifics = better search visibility.
  5. Write title (80 chars max) — [Year] [Brand] [Set/Insert] [Player] [Card #] [Parallel] [Feature]
    Example: 2024 Topps Chrome Refractor Elly De La Cruz #150 RC Rookie
    No "LOOK!", "GREAT DEAL!", or "L@@K" — these hurt ranking.
  6. Add photos — 4–12 from your lightbox. Front, back, corners/edges close-ups. First photo = search thumbnail. 1600px+ minimum.
  7. Write description — brief: condition notes, flaws, shipping method, what they receive. Use a reusable HTML template.
  8. Set price & format — Buy It Now + Best Offer for most. Set auto-accept (80%+) and auto-decline (under 60%). Auction for rare/hyped cards ending Sunday 7–9 PM EST.
  9. Apply shipping profile — select your saved template
  10. Review and publish

Bulk Listing (Efficiency)

eBay Magical Bulk Listing Tool (Free, Built-in)

Where: Seller Hub → Listings → Create Listings → "Multiple listings from photos"

How: Upload a batch of card photos. eBay's AI generates draft listings with suggested categories, titles, item specifics, and pricing. Review each draft, correct errors, publish.

Key detail: Specify how many photos per card (e.g., 2 or 4). Use exactly that number per card so the AI groups them correctly.

Best for: Moderate batches (dozens). Still requires manual review — AI may misidentify cards.

CSV Bulk Upload

Where: Seller Hub → Listings → Create Listings → "Upload from file"

Download eBay's CSV template, fill columns for each card. Can process hundreds/thousands at once. Steep learning curve — template is complex. Photo upload is separate.

When to Grade — ROI Formula

Grade only if the math works:
(Expected graded sale) − (Raw value) − (Grading fee) − (Shipping to grader) − (Platform fees on graded price) = Profit
  • Only grade if PSA 9/10 yields at least 3x raw value after all fees
  • At PSA $79.99: card needs to gain $120+ in value from grading
  • At CGC $17 (bulk): threshold drops to ~$40+ gain needed
  • Never grade a card worth less than $20 raw
  • Don't grade damaged cards hoping for a miracle — PSA 6 or below rarely adds value for modern

Self-Grading Before Submission

The four pillars (use a loupe or magnifying glass):

  • Centering — PSA 10 requires 60/40 or better front, 75/25 or better back
  • Corners — all four must be sharp. Look for fuzzing, dings, soft corners under magnification
  • Edges — no chipping, roughness, or white showing along colored edges
  • Surface — examine under direct light at multiple angles. No scratches, print defects, wax stains, creases

eBay Authenticity Guarantee

Cards $200+ go through PSA authentication (free — eBay covers it). You ship to PSA's center, they verify authenticity and condition consistency, then forward to buyer. Cards that fail are returned to you.

Shipping & Packaging

Packaging: Raw Single Cards

  1. Penny sleeve — slide card in, opening facing up
  2. Top loader (35pt standard) — insert sleeved card, opening at top
  3. Team bag — seal the top loader inside
  4. Painter's tape — one strip across top loader opening to prevent sliding. Never tape directly on a card.
  5. Cardboard sandwich — top loader between two pieces of stiff cardboard, secured with rubber band or painter's tape
  6. Bubble mailer — place sandwich inside, seal

Packaging: Graded Slabs

  1. Photograph front, back, and label before packing
  2. Slab sleeve — prevents scuffs on the acrylic case
  3. Bubble wrap — 2+ layers of small-bubble wrap, secure with painter's tape
  4. Cardboard sandwich — both sides, slightly oversized, bound with rubber bands
  5. Snug box — the slab must NOT move when you shake the box. Fill all gaps.
  6. Priority Mail — ship with tracking + insurance

Sourcing Deals

eBay Sourcing

  • Auction sniping — Auctions ending Tue–Thu, 9am–12pm draw the least competition (20–40% below market). Use Gixen or EZSniper to bid in the final seconds.
  • Typo hunting — Misspelled names get fewer views = less competition. Search "Charzard", "Othani", etc. FatFingers tool helps find these.
  • Bulk lots — Unsorted collections are often underpriced. Buy lots, pull valuable singles, resell rest as smaller lots.
  • Saved Searches — Set email alerts for specific players/sets at target prices.
  • Best Offer strategy — Offer 50–60% on BIN listings sitting 30+ days.

Facebook & Craigslist

  • Search Marketplace and Craigslist for "sports cards", "card collection", "baseball cards lot"
  • Join local BST groups — people sell collections from deceased relatives, downsizing
  • Set up notifications for new posts matching your terms
  • Negotiate hard on Craigslist — start at 40–50% of asking. Sellers expect it.

In-Person Sourcing

  • Estate sales — #1 source for underpriced cards. Families often don't know card values. Use EstateSales.net and GarageSaleTracker apps.
  • Thrift stores — Check toys/games section. Ask employees about card donations.
  • Card shows — Best deals in the last hour when dealers want to move inventory vs pack up.
  • Local card shops — Build relationships. Ask about bulk or dollar boxes with hidden gems.
  • Pawn shops / antique stores — Cards aren't their specialty = priced below market.
Sourcing Rules
Always check comps on your phone before buying (eBay Sold or 130point.com). Factor in platform fees (~13% eBay, ~10% Mercari). Target cards worth $5+ individually — below that, sell as lots. Junk wax era (1987–1993) Topps, Donruss, Fleer, Score are generally worthless in bulk.

Multi-Channel Hub Strategy

The goal: own a canonical catalog where every card has one identity, and every marketplace listing is just a projection of that identity. When a card sells anywhere, it disappears everywhere else within minutes.

The Core Problem with Unique Items
Most cards are quantity-one items. If the same card is listed on eBay, Mercari, and Fanatics simultaneously, and two buyers purchase on different platforms before you can react, you've double-sold a card you only have one of. That means a cancellation, negative feedback, and potential account issues. Your inventory system must treat this as the #1 risk to solve.

The Control Plane Concept

Don't think of your hub as "another store." Think of it as a control plane — you own the canonical catalog, inventory ledger, pricing rules, and media. Marketplaces receive a projection of your data, not the original.

DataWho Owns It
SKU / product identity / photosYour hub (always)
Cost / acquisition dataYour hub only — never expose
Description, collectible metadataYour hub
Physical inventory countYour hub inventory ledger
Base priceYour hub
Channel-specific title/price overridesYour hub, per-channel
External listing ID / statusThe channel
Buyer / order identityOriginating channel
Marketplace fees / tax chargedThe channel / order
Fulfillment / trackingYour hub after shipping, pushed out

Inventory Ledger (Not Just a Number)

For unique cards, model inventory as a ledger, not a single mutable number:

Available to Sell = On Hand − Reserved − Safety Stock
On hand1
Reserved (pending sale)0
Safety stock0
Available to sell1

When a paid order event arrives from ANY channel: atomically create a reservation and set ATS to 0 before doing anything else. Then fan out "quantity = 0" or "end listing" commands to every other channel. This doesn't eliminate double-sells (two platforms can accept orders before either event reaches you), but it minimizes the window.

For rare/high-value one-of-one cards, also support:

  • Withholding from lower-priority channels entirely
  • A "sell lock" that suppresses all other listings after a committed transaction
  • A one-click "deactivate everywhere" emergency command

Channel-Specific Pricing Formula

Don't set prices manually per platform. Use a formula so every channel price accounts for its specific fee structure:

Channel Price Formula
Channel Price = (Base Price + Fixed Costs + Shipping Recovery) ÷ (1 − Variable Fee %)
Example: $40 target proceeds
Base target proceeds$40.00
Shipping recovery$4.50
Fixed buffer$0.50
eBay variable fee14%
eBay listing price$52.33
($40 + $4.50 + $0.50) ÷ 0.86 = $52.33

Pricing rules should follow a precedence chain:

  1. Manual SKU/channel override (you force a specific price)
  2. Campaign rule (sale, promotion)
  3. Category/channel rule (all baseball cards on eBay get +5%)
  4. Default channel rule (eBay = base + 14% fee recovery)
  5. Canonical base price

Phase 1 Start Simple (0–200 Listings)

  • Platforms: eBay (primary) + Mercari + Facebook Marketplace
  • Cross-listing: Vendoo ($8.99/mo) — list on eBay first, one-click cross-list
  • Inventory: Google Sheets or Vendoo's built-in tracking
  • Monthly cost: ~$9 + platform fees on sales
  • Double-sell prevention: Vendoo auto-delist on some plans. Manual delist as backup.

Phase 2 Expand (200–1,000 Listings)

  • Add: Fanatics Collect (6% fees) + MySlabs (graded) + Etsy (vintage/memorabilia)
  • Cross-listing: Nembol ($39/mo for 500 products) or List Perfectly — broader platform support + API access on higher tiers
  • Card tools: Mascot (PSA/BGS/SGC/CGC integration) for grading-specific workflows
  • Monthly cost: ~$40–$80 in tools
  • Consider: Whatnot (if on camera), TikTok Shop (if making content)

Phase 3 Build the Hub (1,000+ Listings)

  • Central hub: Your own website (Shopify as transaction engine, NOT as master database)
  • Key distinction: Your hub owns product identity, metadata, costs, inventory, pricing rules, analytics. Shopify receives a "sellable projection" and handles cart/checkout/payments.
  • eBay sync: CedCommerce ($29+/mo) for Shopify ↔ eBay auto-sync
  • Cross-list: Vendoo/Nembol for Mercari, FB, Etsy
  • Monthly cost: ~$100–$170 (Shopify $39 + CedCommerce $29 + Nembol/Vendoo $39)
  • Future migration: Because your hub is the master, you could swap Shopify for BigCommerce or WooCommerce by changing one adapter — not rebuilding everything.

The Hub Architecture

YOUR CONTROL PLANE (canonical catalog + inventory ledger) | |-- eBay via CedCommerce — auto-sync (full API) |-- Shopify / Your Site direct adapter — 0% marketplace fees |-- Mercari via Vendoo/Nembol — cross-list |-- FB Marketplace via Vendoo — cross-list |-- Fanatics Collect manual (no public API yet) |-- Etsy via Nembol or direct (Open API v3) |-- TikTok Shop via adapter (TikTok Open API) |-- MySlabs manual — graded only |-- Whatnot manual — live stream format |-- Poshmark CSV export only — no API
The Golden Rule
When a card sells on ANY platform, it must be removed from ALL others within minutes. Auto-delist tools handle some of this. For platforms without auto-sync, your first action after a sale notification is: mark sold in your master inventory, which triggers removal everywhere else. For high-value one-of-one cards, consider listing on only one platform at a time.

Buy commodity connectivity (Vendoo/Nembol for cross-listing), build the control plane (your canonical catalog, inventory ledger, pricing rules). Use a SaaS bridge for secondary channels, then replace with direct adapters only when the SaaS creates oversell risk, can't represent your data, or costs more than maintaining your own adapter.

When to Build callunc.com (Custom Platform)

At high volume (1,000+ active listings, multi-channel), a custom control plane becomes the long-term play:

  • You own: canonical catalog, SKU/asset data, inventory ledger, channel mappings, pricing rules, order normalization, analytics
  • Adapters publish to: eBay (Inventory API), Shopify (GraphQL Admin API), Etsy (Open API v3), TikTok (Open API), Amazon (SP-API)
  • Shopify handles: storefront checkout only — not the master database
  • Stack: TypeScript/Node.js backend, PostgreSQL, Redis, SQS/event bus, S3 for media, Next.js admin dashboard
  • MVP: eBay + callunc.com/Shopify first. Prove "sell once, disappear everywhere" before adding channels.
Cost Reality
A hybrid approach (your catalog + direct eBay/Shopify adapters + SaaS bridge for others) runs ~$120K–$250K in engineering effort. A full custom platform with most channels direct is $300K–$700K+. Start with Phase 1–2 using off-the-shelf tools, and graduate to custom only when volume and margin justify it.

Taxes & Record Keeping

1099-K Reporting

  • Federal: Platforms report at $20,000 AND 200+ transactions (verify — phased threshold may be $5,000 in 2026)
  • Many states use $600 regardless — you may get a 1099-K even with modest sales
  • All income is taxable whether or not you receive a 1099-K

Hobby vs Business

HobbyBusiness (Schedule C)
Gains taxable? Yes Yes
Expenses deductible? Generally no (TCJA suspension) Yes — shipping, supplies, grading fees, equipment, travel to shows, home office
Self-employment tax? No ~15.3% on profits
IRS test 9 factors — record-keeping, expertise, time, profit history, intent. Rule of thumb: show profit 3 of 5 years.
Recommendation
If selling regularly (not just clearing a personal collection), file as a business from the start. Deductions (grading fees, supplies, shipping, equipment, card show travel) usually outweigh the self-employment tax hit.

Record Keeping

Track every transaction: date, platform, item description, sale price, platform fees, shipping cost, original purchase price (your cost basis), net profit. Keep receipts for all supplies, grading fees, shipping, and inventory purchases.

Consider a separate bank account for card sales to keep finances clean.
